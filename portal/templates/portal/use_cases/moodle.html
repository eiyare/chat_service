The answers are in Moodle. Students still can't find them.

Dates, instructions, rubrics and grades are spread across sections, files, forums and the gradebook. So students ask their teachers, at all hours, about the same few things.

When is the lab report due?

The date is in the assignment, the schedule and a forum post. Every class asks anyway, and teachers answer it again each time.

What do I need to pass?

Can we trust AI near our courses?

Answers from the course itself

Grounded in your course, with the source cited

The problem

Generic AI tools guess. They don't know your syllabus, your dates or your marking scheme, and they make things up with confidence.

What it does

Every hour, the plugin sends each course you choose: the overview and schedule, activity descriptions, pages and books, files, and forum posts. On a course's pages, students get answers from that course only, each one citing where it came from. If the course doesn't say, the assistant says so instead of inventing an answer.

  • Only what every student can already see is sent. Hidden or restricted sections, private replies and quiz questions never are.
  • Only changes are re-sent, and deleted items are removed.
  • Each course keeps its own conversation, so Biology answers never mix with History.

Personal progress

“What's my grade?” answered in seconds, not after an email

The problem

Students don't know where they stand until it's too late to act. Working out what's left and what they need means piecing together the gradebook, completion rules and due dates, so they email the teacher instead.

What it does

When a student asks about their own progress, the assistant looks it up in Moodle as that student, possibly several things in turn, and answers in plain words.

  • Grades: each item, the course total, weights, feedback, and how much of the course is still to come.
  • Completion: the course's completion criteria, which ones are met, and what each activity still requires.
  • Deadlines: what's left to do and when, including the student's own extensions, with anything overdue flagged.

Knows where the student is

“This module” means the one they're looking at

The problem

Students ask the way people talk: “what's this about?”, “and the second one?”, “yes, tell me more”. Chatbots that don't know the page or follow the conversation answer the wrong question.

What it does

The assistant knows which course and activity the student has open, reads that page first, and follows the conversation, so follow-ups and short replies are understood.

  • Greets signed-in students by name.
  • Conversations follow students across devices.
  • Understands English and Nigerian Pidgin, and replies in kind.

Assessment integrity

Present for learning. Absent during exams.

The problem

An assistant that is available during a quiz becomes a cheating tool, and one that appears everywhere becomes noise.

What it does

You decide where the assistant appears: site home, dashboard, course pages, activities, quiz attempts, reports, admin pages, the login page. Untick Quiz attempts and it disappears while students answer quizzes, including in Safe Exam Browser.

  • Choose which courses take part: all visible courses, or a list.
  • Hide it on specific page types, such as every assignment page.
  • Quiz questions are never sent, whatever you choose.

Privacy by design

Student records stay under Moodle's rules

The problem

Schools are accountable for student data. Handing a third-party tool broad access to grades is a risk most leaders can't accept, and shouldn't.

What it does

There is no admin key that can read everyone's grades. A lookup only works with the asking student's own sign-in, and Moodle runs it as that student, so it sees exactly what the student would see, and nothing more.

  • Student data lookups are off until you switch them on, in both Moodle and {{ SITE_NAME }}.
  • Hidden grades, and grades not yet released, stay hidden.
  • The assistant only connects to your own Moodle sites, and the plugin declares what it sends through Moodle's privacy API.
  • Looked-up records are used for the answer and aren't kept in {{ SITE_NAME }}'s reports.

Transparency

See exactly how every answer was produced

The problem

When a parent or teacher questions an answer, “the AI said so” isn't good enough. Most tools are a black box.

What it does

The Brain map shows each step behind every reply: how the question was understood, which course passages were considered, kept or dropped and why, what was looked up, what the model was sent, and how long each step took.

  • Open any conversation and follow its reasoning step by step.
  • Lookups are recorded as what was fetched, not the student's grades.
  • Every failure is recorded with its cause, so problems get fixed rather than guessed at.

Insight and cost control

Know what students ask, and what it costs

The problem

You can't improve what you can't see, and AI costs that grow without limit are hard to budget for.

What it does

A performance report shows how many questions students ask, how many are answered, how fast, and what it costs. It also lists the questions it couldn't answer, so you know exactly which document or FAQ to add next.

  • A usage allowance per day or month keeps spending predictable.
  • Common questions can be answered instantly from FAQs you write.
  • If the AI provider has a brief outage, the assistant retries automatically.

Questions schools ask

Will students use it to cheat?

You can hide it on quiz attempt pages, including in Safe Exam Browser, and quiz questions are never sent to it. Outside quizzes it is built to answer from your course material, with sources, rather than as a general-purpose writing tool. Every conversation can be reviewed in the Brain map.

What student data leaves Moodle?

For greeting and conversations: the student's Moodle ID, name, and their questions. If you switch on student data: that student's own grades, completion and deadlines, looked up while they ask and used to answer them. Course content includes only what every student can already see. All of this is declared through Moodle's privacy API.

Can it see a student's hidden grades, or another student's grades?

No. Each lookup needs the asking student's own sign-in, and Moodle runs it as that student, so it gets exactly what they could see in their own grade report.

What happens when it doesn't know?

It says so, rather than guessing. Those questions appear in your performance report, so you can add the missing document or an FAQ.

Which Moodle versions are supported?

Moodle 4.0 and later. It installs as a standard local plugin through Moodle's plugin installer, with no changes to Moodle's core code or your theme.

Do teachers need to set anything up?

No. It reads what's already in each course. Teachers who keep dates and instructions in Moodle get better answers automatically.

How is it priced?

You can start free and try it with your own material. Usage allowances keep costs predictable as you roll it out to more courses.
